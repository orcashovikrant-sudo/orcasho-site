What is missing, in plain words
Each card below takes one row of the table above. It gives the gap in one sentence, a real-world picture of one person, and two flow charts. The top flow shows how things work today and where they get stuck. The green flow shows the same story with the missing layer in place, ending with where that industry is headed. The people in the pictures are illustrations, not patients.
GI acid suppressionMissing: A step-down path
In plain words
Starting an acid pill is easy. Coming off it safely is not, so people stay on it for years.
A real-world picture: Meena
Meena takes an acid pill every day for heartburn. After a few months she decides to stop. For a week or two the heartburn comes back worse than before. She thinks, "I clearly need this forever," and restarts. Nothing in her care helped her step down slowly.
Today: the gap
1
Takes the pill daily
→
2
Heartburn goes away
→
3
Stops the pill
→
4
Rebound heartburn hits
→
5
Restarts, for years
With the missing layer: the same story, played forward
1
Meena's pills, food and feedback are loaded
→
2
Simulator reads where in the gut each change acts
→
3
Dose steps down in small, supported steps
→
4
Feedback comes back every 20 days
→
5
Care team adjusts the next step
→
6
Off the pill, or on the lowest dose
Where this is going. Every patient who comes off a pill adds to a map. The next patient gets a safer path than the last.
Who picks it up: Health systems, GI and OTC players
AI drug discoveryMissing: Response after the molecule exists
In plain words
AI can design a molecule. Nobody can yet show how a real person's body responds to it over months.
A real-world picture: A lab
A lab's AI finds a molecule that works well on cells and in animals. In the human trial, many people react in ways no model saw, because they eat different food and take other medicines. The trial fails late, after years and a lot of money.
Today: the gap
1
AI finds a molecule
→
2

→
3

→
4
Real people react in unseen ways
→
5
Late and costly failure
With the missing layer: the same story, played forward
1
The molecule enters as an input
→
2
Simulator reads it gate by gate, with food and medicines
→
3
It predicts who it suits and why
→
4
Tested in the 20-day loop
→
5
The real response returns as data
→
6
The next molecule starts smarter
Where this is going. Discovery teams will check human response early, before the expensive trial, not after it.
Who picks it up: Discovery platforms, pharma R&D
PharmaMissing: Patient-specific context
In plain words
A drug is built for one job and one dose. Real people also eat, take other pills and have different gut chemistry.
A real-world picture: Arjun
Arjun takes a new tablet with his regular antacid and his usual breakfast. His friend takes the same tablet on an empty stomach. It works well for one of them and barely for the other. The label only says "take with food." It cannot cover every combination.
Today: the gap
1
Drug approved for one job
→
2
Label gives a general rule
→
3
Patient adds food and other pills
→
4
Nobody checks the mix
→
5
Same drug, different results
With the missing layer: the same story, played forward
1
The drug becomes a known input
→
2
This patient's food and medicines are added
→
3
Simulator reads the combined response
→
4
Care team picks timing and companions
→
5
Feedback returns each cycle
→
6
A drug, food and combination map grows
Where this is going. Drug makers will ship a "how it behaves in real life" layer alongside the label.
Who picks it up: GI and metabolic franchises
MicrobiomeMissing: The host environment
In plain words
A stool test lists which bacteria are present. It does not explain why they act differently in different people.
A real-world picture: Two friends
Two friends take the same probiotic. One feels much better. The other feels nothing. Their tests show the same bacteria. The difference is the acidity, bile and nutrients where the probiotic lands, and nobody measured that.
Today: the gap
1
Stool test lists bacteria
→
2
Same probiotic for both
→
3
One person responds
→
4
The other does not
→
5
Nobody knows why
With the missing layer: the same story, played forward
1
The strain becomes an input
→
2
Each person's gut chemistry is read
→
3
People are grouped by chemistry
→
4
The strain is matched to the group it suits
→
5
Responses are logged
→
6
Developers know exactly who to enrol
Where this is going. Strain developers will recruit by gut chemistry, so more trials succeed and fewer people get a product that cannot work for them.
Who picks it up: Live-biotherapeutic developers
SupplementsMissing: An outcome loop and interaction model

Priya answers a quiz and gets a personalised monthly pack. Two ingredients in it interfere with each other in her gut, so one is barely absorbed. She feels no change. The company never hears about it and ships the same pack next month.

Diagnostics, RWD, twinsMissing: The intervention column
In plain words

A real-world picture: Rohan
Rohan's lab number improves over a year. The record shows the improvement. It does not show that he changed his diet, dropped one pill and tried two supplements along the way. Nobody can say what actually helped.

Personalised nutritionMissing: Food as chemistry
In plain words
Diet plans hand out rules. They do not treat food as something with a measurable effect.
A real-world picture: Kavya
Kavya follows her diet plan carefully. At a family wedding she eats outside it and has a flare. Her app marks it as a failure. In truth it was useful data about what that meal does to her gut, and nobody used it.
