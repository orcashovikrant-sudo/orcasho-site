The core idea

Every chronic-care system treats gastro tract dysfunction as a symptom to suppress. We treat it as a control variable to manage continuously.

That single move, from treatment target to control variable, makes a feedback loop possible where a static protocol never can be.

The objective

Patient stability despite food, medication, hormones and travel.

Typical chronic care: the patient adapts

Restrict food. Avoid triggers. Change lifestyle. Re-dose medication. Result: fragile stability that breaks when life varies.

Orcasho: the protocol adapts

Life keeps varying. A 20-day feedback cycle re-adjusts the protocol. Result: a consistent response, day after day.

“Patients should not need to organize their lives around their condition. The system should organize itself around the patient.”

Mechanism

The gastro tract is upstream. Everything else flows from it.

Upstream: what we controlDigestive and absorption state: enzymatic environment, mucosal integrity, motility and transit, ionic equilibrium (15 markers)
›
The 20-day loopEvery cycle: patient feedback, biochemical profile update, next intervention selected

Downstream: what changesMedication absorption, blood markers, hormonal balance (TSH, lipids), tolerance of food variation

In plain terms: if digestion is broken, nothing else can be fixed reliably. Medicines don’t absorb properly, blood markers stay erratic, hormones stay off. Fix the gastro tract first and everything downstream gets easier.

The feedback loop

Not a protocol. A control system.

Before the first prescription

The simulator has the history of all earlier patients and predicts prescription 1 from it.

Every prescription after that

The simulator runs on that history plus this patient’s own past, predicts the next combination, and real feedback joins the patient’s record.

Think thermostat, not prescription pad. A doctor reviews and signs each prescription.
