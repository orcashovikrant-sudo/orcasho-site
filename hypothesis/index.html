How the model updates itself+

We start with a working hypothesis about why a patient is unstable. Every 20-day cycle of real-world observation either reinforces it or corrects it, that's the whole loop, repeated all year.

Repeated often enough, this is how a working hypothesis becomes a reliable model: not by assuming it's true, but by testing it against real life, again and again.

Technical implementation

Internally, the model represents each patient using a multi-dimensional biochemical state space that incorporates our working hypothesis around digestive physiology, biochemical interactions, and ionic equilibrium. This representation is continuously updated as longitudinal observations accumulate.

Why we start with the gut+
Gastrointestinal tract

It's a practical entry point (measurable, clinically relevant) and it continuously influences everyday physiology. This is where we start, not a claim that every disease begins here.

What compounds over time+

Existing medicine manages disease. Orcasho continuously adapts physiological stability between clinical encounters, and that repeatability doesn't stay clinical. It compounds into the economics too.

Clinical repeatability is the hinge. Once outcomes repeat reliably, patients stay on the programme and refer others. That retention and referral flow is what turns into recurring revenue and a lower cost of growth.

That revenue and reach fund more longitudinal data collection, which sharpens the Adaptive Physiological State Model further, which makes outcomes repeat even more reliably, the same loop, reinforcing itself.

Downstream, patients also tend to see symptoms and food tolerance improve, need less symptom-relief medication, and show improvement on some routine markers (clinical evidence, not the primary claim, but part of what makes the loop repeat).
