How the model works, step by step

This page is a simplified view of the full Orcasho system. The real engine works with 15-dimensional ionic vectors and four pH-defined digestive gates. It can evaluate many candidate combinations, including thousands of possible combinations, and compare the resulting simulations to identify which direction is closest to the target.

1 · Define the targetFirst define the state we want to move toward. In this example, the stomach target is lower H+ and higher HCO3−.
2 · Represent each compoundEach compound is represented as a vector. Here we use only two dimensions: its effect on H+ and HCO3−.
3 · Move the compoundMove the H+ and HCO3− controls to change the direction and magnitude of that compound's ionic effect. The arrow moves with it.
4 · Compare to the targetCosine similarity compares the compound's direction with the target direction. A higher positive cosine means closer directional alignment.
5 · Combine compoundsGive Compound A and Compound B different weights. The system calculates a weighted combination, creating a new vector and therefore a new arrow.
6 · Run the simulationIn the full model, the candidate combination is propagated through the four digestive gates, where pH-dependent chemistry changes the resulting vector at each gate.
7 · Repeat across candidatesMany different combinations and weightings can be simulated. The resulting arrows and cosine scores can be compared against the same target.
8 · Compare and refineYou can compare two combinations, two simulation runs, or many candidate combinations and identify which one produces the stronger target-aligned direction.
What is being compared? Combination 1 → simulate → target alignment Combination 2 → simulate → target alignment Many candidates → simulate → rank by directional alignment

Important: the arrows shown below are a teaching representation. The actual system performs the same directional logic across the full 15-dimensional ionic vectors and the four digestive gates. BEFORE THE SIMULATION

Where the empirical percentages enter

The simulation does not create the empirical weights from scratch. They come from the accumulated prescription-response record that sits upstream of the simulation.

01Real prescriptionsRepeated treatment combinations create the observed prescription history and frequencies.
02Prescription-response learningA local Random Forest learns recurring relationships between prescription patterns and the response signals that follow them.
03Python-derived inputsPrescription frequencies, recurring combinations and associated response weights are converted into structured inputs.
04Then the simulationThose empirical inputs inform the candidate combination that is propagated through the four digestive gates.
Key distinction: the percentages are derived from the real prescription-response record. The mechanistic simulation uses those structured empirical inputs to evaluate candidate interventions; it does not invent the percentages.
FROM PATIENT STATE TO THE NEXT PRESCRIPTION

Orcasho does not treat a prescription as a fixed endpoint. Every 20-day cycle starts with the patient's current state, evaluates a candidate intervention, simulates how the combination moves through the digestive environment, and uses the resulting state to inform the next prescription.

WHY THE LOOP MATTERS

Every cycle creates another prescription-to-feedback loop

The intervention layer is not a one-time simulation. Each 20-day cycle produces another observation linking a patient state to a prescription and its response.

→
02 Prescription Candidate combination selected for the cycle

Repeat the loop → more intervention-response sequences → more context for the next prescription. This is the foundation of the intervention layer: the system is not simply storing patient data. It is generating a longitudinal sequence of interventions and responses that can be compared, simulated and used to refine subsequent decisions.
Public demonstration boundary. The page shows the simulation structure, the four-gate path, ionic movement and the comparison of candidate combinations. The underlying architecture, mappings, production parameters, thresholds and prescription-selection rules are intentionally not disclosed.
And this is where cosine similarity fits: the simulation produces the resulting ionic state; cosine similarity measures how closely that resulting direction aligns with the target. Candidate combinations can therefore be compared before the next 20-day prescription.
