02, The gap

The patient spends most of the year
outside the health system.

In a typical chronic-care journey, patients may interact with their health system for roughly 25 days of the year. For the remaining ~340 days, they are living with the condition outside the clinical environment, while their symptoms, diet, medication response, absorption, and day-to-day physiology continue to change.

This is the space Orcasho is designed to operate in.

~25 days/year
~340 days/year, patient living outside the health system
Orcasho provides a continuous longitudinal intervention layer during those 340 days. Every 20 days, the patient's response is reviewed and the next intervention cycle is adjusted, with the resulting patient data continuously available to the physician.

The same pattern exists across chronic care. Patients are seen at defined clinical touchpoints, but most of their year is spent managing their condition outside the clinic. Orcasho is designed to provide the continuous monitoring, intervention, and feedback loop during that period, while keeping the physician informed and in control.

03, How the model fits

The B2C model was not built
for this gap. It just fits it.

This is the same execution layer we already run in B2C, every twenty days. The only real difference is who is watching it happen. There, it's us. Here, it's also the physician.

Value-based networks are built around a very different cadence. A patient typically visits a physician once or twice a quarter. Across the remaining days of the year, there is no structured touchpoint at all. Orcasho does not add a workflow or an administrative layer on top of that visit cadence. It moves the entire execution layer into the patient's home, runs the twenty-day intervention cycle there, and updates a physician-facing dashboard continuously, so the physician keeps full visibility and control without carrying the execution burden themselves.

Most patient care moves outside the hospital and into the home. Visit frequency drops. Physician visibility does not.

04, The pathway

One system,
two markets.

The B2C base, including patients already in the United States, is the evidence. The value-based care networks below are where that evidence becomes a pilot. Reaching each one follows the same motion: identify the innovation, partnerships, or population health team inside the organization, open with the specific insight about the ~340 days patients spend outside the health system rather than a generic pitch, and follow up with the physician-facing pilot walkthrough we have already built. Where a conference introduction exists, that is the first move. Where it does not, the entry point is a direct approach to the team running chronic care management or value-based contracts.
