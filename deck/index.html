In plain terms: we turn a patient's body into 15 numbers instead of a vague symptom list so "better" or "worse" becomes something we can actually measure.
Layer 2 Selection

Compound selection via cosine similarity

Pick the compounds whose ionic direction points closest to the target.

target_vector = Σ(symptom_vectors)
score = cosine_similarity(compound_ions, target)
protocol = top_k(score, library)
In plain terms: the system does the matching math for you picking whichever combination of safe compounds points closest to where that patient needs to go.
Layer 3 Memory

Within-patient intervention-response history

Each cycle adds what changed and what worked. 10 to 20 cycles build a fingerprint.

In plain terms: the system remembers every patient's history and uses it a new competitor starting today has zero of this memory, no matter how much money they raise.

09 - The Intelligence Curve

Three thresholds.
Each unlocks something the last couldn't do.

Patient count isn't just a traction metric. At each threshold the accumulated longitudinal dataset crosses a qualitative capability boundary.

300

Working system

Individual response patterns visible. Proof that the upstream hypothesis holds across diverse demographics, conditions, and geographies. This is where we are now.

Who values it: Seed and Series A investors, clinical partners, first health-system pilots. To prove at this stage: reproducible benefit in the GI beachhead (PPI step-down, reflux, bloating), with dropouts included.

3K

Pattern recognition layer emerges

Cross-patient biochemical similarity becomes queryable. The system begins predicting which protocol modifications will work for a new patient based on profile matching with prior patients.

Who values it: Licensing and pilot revenue from consumer-health groups, GI and metabolic pharma, nutrition brands. To prove: prediction that beats a static protocol on held-out patients.

30K

Predictive infrastructure

The longitudinal intervention-response dataset becomes licensable - to health systems, payers, pharma companies modeling real-world supplement-drug interaction patterns. The data layer acquires value independent of any single patient interaction.

Who values it: Strategic acquirers or multi-year licensees: pharma, AI drug discovery, RWD and digital-twin platforms, microbiome and consumer-health majors. To prove: one externally owned molecule integrated prospectively.
