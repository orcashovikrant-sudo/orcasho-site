Company

What we do, in one sentence

Orcasho is a closed-loop control system for chronic care that treats the gastro tract as the upstream control point and re-adjusts each patient’s supplement protocol every 20 days from real-world feedback, with no dietary restriction.

What we do, in a paragraph

Orcasho is a closed-loop control system for chronic care that treats the gastro tract as the upstream control point instead of a symptom to suppress. If digestion and absorption are off, medicines don’t absorb reliably, blood markers stay erratic and hormones drift, so we stabilize that layer first. Each patient is modeled as a 15-number ionic state. A locally run simulator, built on a Random Forest trained on our prescription-to-response record plus a mechanistic four-gate model (stomach, duodenum, small intestine, colon), picks the next combination from a library of 101 compounds. A doctor reviews and signs every prescription. Real feedback comes back every 20 days, is read by our own language model, and the loop runs again, with no dietary restriction for the patient. Nothing in the decision layer touches an external API or third-party model. We’ve been running since January 2022 with about 300 patients across India and the US, 100% B2C and bootstrapped, with 1,510+ feedback entries over 46 months. 96% of patients have cut down or stopped their PPI, gross margin is 75%, and 38% have reordered, with 39% of growth coming from unincentivized patient referral. The moat is time: every retained patient adds 9 to 24 months of intervention and response data that can’t be scraped or bought. We’re raising a $2M seed to reach 3,000 patients, add a US clinical lead and pilot partner, and run prospective outcomes studies.

Founders and Team

My story, and why I’m the one to build this

How I came to this idea, and the unfair advantage I bring to it.

I didn’t set out to start a company. I trained as a physical chemist, where you learn to think in ions, pH and equilibria, and I started applying that thinking to digestion. I wanted to know whether gut dysfunction could be treated as a changing biochemical system instead of a list of symptoms to suppress. At first I just helped people I knew with supplement combinations that I adjusted based on how they responded. Over three to four years, some of them started paying me to continue, and that’s when I saw it could be a real business. I incorporated Orcasho in January 2022 and launched in January 2023.

What kept me on it was how stuck these patients were. Most had been on PPIs for 6, 10 or even 15 years and had already tried doctors, dietitians and microbiome tests. Chronic care is also one visit and then silence, so patients spend about 340 days a year on their own while their food, medicines and travel keep changing. A fixed protocol can’t keep up with that. It’s a control problem, and control problems are what I was trained to solve.

My unfair advantage is four things that compound:

  • A search space too large to copy. Every prescription is a combination of at least 5 supplements from our library of 101 compounds. That gives about 79 million possible 5-compound combinations, and over 9 billion if order mattered. It’s far more once you allow 6 or more compounds per prescription. A patient gets a new combination every 20 days, so a 9 to 24 month stay means roughly 14 to 36 prescriptions, and some patients stay 2 to 3 years. Against a space that size, nobody can find what works by trial and error or by copying a protocol. You need the four-gate chemistry to narrow the space and real patient history to show which paths inside it actually work. Every cycle adds to that history.
  • Data nobody else has. Orcasho has about 300 patients and 1510+ feedback entries over 46 months, with 9 to 24 months of intervention and response per retained patient. None of it is on the internet, so it can’t be scraped, licensed or bought.
  • Everything runs locally, and the IP is ours. Prescription decoding, the Random Forest analysis, the language analysis of patient feedback and the four-gate simulator all run on our own systems. No external API or third-party model is called, and cloud AI never touches the decision layer. We also built our own language model, which follows the Orcasho framework and reads patient feedback. The encoding, thresholds, scoring, parameters and selection rules are all protected IP. A doctor reviews and signs every prescription, and medicine changes are always the doctor’s call, never the model’s.
  • The right training and proof it works. The core is a 15-dimensional ionic model, which is physical chemistry and not a generic ML wrapper. I was a Marie Skłodowska-Curie ITN Fellow (EU Horizon 2020) with research at Argonne National Laboratory and a 2019 ACS paper, and my peers elected me their representative. When our engineer left because we ran out of cash, I taught myself HTML, JavaScript and Python and rebuilt the whole system, using Claude as a coding partner while I set the architecture and the biochemical logic. Commercially, 96% of patients have cut down or stopped their PPI, 38% reorder (112 of 293), and we get 0.91 repeat orders per new patient (267 repeat orders from 293 first orders). 39% of growth comes from referral, gross margin is 75%, and a retained patient returns 7x our acquisition cost, recovered in under a month.

Product, Technology, and Business

How we built it

The technology, what makes it hard to build, and our moat.

We built it ourselves, without a technical co-founder. The operations side is an HTML and JavaScript app on Firebase that handles patients, consultations and the doctor dashboard. The core is a local Python engine. It takes the prescription-and-feedback record, runs a Random Forest to find recurring prescription-to-response patterns, and turns those into inputs for a four-gate simulation (stomach, duodenum, small intestine, colon) built on a 15-number ionic state for each patient. We also built our own language model to read patient feedback. Everything in the decision layer runs on our own systems with no external API. Cloud AI is used only for reminders, support triage and scheduling. A doctor reviews and signs every prescription.

Two things make it hard. First, every prescription combines at least 5 of our 101 compounds, which gives about 79 million possible combinations, so you can’t find what works by trial and error. The chemistry has to narrow the space and real patient history has to show which paths work. Second, the history only comes from time. Each retained patient takes 9 to 24 months to produce, and money doesn’t speed that up.

That is the moat. Our 1510+ feedback entries over 46 months are not on the internet, so they can’t be scraped or bought. The encoding, thresholds, scoring and models are protected IP. A competitor can copy the 20-day cycle and the dashboard, but not a model calibrated on years of real response.

Who we see as competitors, and why we’re different

We get compared to three groups, and we differ from each. Drug discovery companies find new molecules, while we combine existing safe compounds and re-optimize every 20 days. Generic AI health apps wrap a third-party model around a symptom tracker, while ours is a 15-dimensional state model with its own selection math on our own infrastructure. Probiotic subscriptions and microbiome tests read a stool sample, which only shows the end of the tract after everything upstream has settled. We work at the four gates upstream, and we check results against blood work and endoscopy where patients share them, not just self-report. We also sit alongside PK/PD and PBPK modelling instead of competing with it. Those tools size a dose for an average virtual patient, while we track a real patient over time and flag what needs modelling.

How we make money

Who our customers are, the pain point we solve for them, and how this scales to a multimillion-dollar business.

Today it’s direct revenue from patients, who pay for a recurring 20-day protocol. Pricing is currently ₹8,900 to ₹21,000 (about $94 to $222) per 12-week program, set by age, number of gastro issues, chronicity and medication history. Pricing will change in the future as we scale, including for US patients. The customers are adults with chronic reflux, bloating or constipation for six months or more despite standard care, many on PPIs for 6 to 15 years. They also include people carrying thyroid, cholesterol, diabetes or immunosuppressant medicines whose absorption depends on the gut. The pain point is that standard care gives a fixed protocol to a body whose food, medicines, hormones and travel keep changing, and patients spend about 340 days a year outside the health system. We make the protocol adapt instead of making the patient restrict their life.

Scale comes from three routes that reuse the same engine. B2C in India is live. B2C in the US is next. B2B in the US means pilots with value-based care networks, accountable care organizations and payer innovation teams, where the engine runs the 340 days between visits and updates a physician dashboard. The longer-term route is the intervention layer. As the record grows, it becomes a licensable reference that drug developers, supplement brands, microbiome companies and diagnostics companies can plug their own products into.

What else we want you to know

Our origin story, our unfair advantage, and what is special about the team.

Everyone who touches this problem owns one piece and lacks the same missing piece. PK/PD and PBPK modelling size a single drug for an average virtual patient: a dose goes in and a blood level comes out. They can’t see what else the real patient is taking, so a supplement binding a medicine in the gut goes unseen unless someone adds it. Microbiome tests read a stool sample, which is the end of the tract, and can only report who survived. They can’t say why the same probiotic works in one person and does nothing in another, because the acid, bile and buffering that decide that sit upstream. Drug makers, AI drug discovery and supplement brands have the same gap. They have a molecule or a formula, but nothing that follows a changing human over months.

We built the missing piece. Orcasho describes every drug, supplement and food in one chemical language and reads it at four stops along the gut, so everything a patient takes is seen together. A clash between two products shows up in the same reading. We redo it every 20 days, so we follow the patient when the gut itself changes, for instance as someone steps off a PPI and the stomach starts making acid again. A one-time snapshot can’t do that. Every reading is paired with the patient’s own reply 20 days later, so the record shows what was tried, in what state and what happened next. That is why this is hard to copy and why it grows. Anyone with an outside molecule, strain or food can plug into the same language, and every partner makes the model better for the next one. We don’t replace PK/PD or microbiome testing. We tell them which question to ask, and we learn from the answer.
