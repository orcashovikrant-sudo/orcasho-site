ORCASHO · CLINICAL SCIENCE NOTE

Same reactant,
different room.

A twenty-day gastrointestinal protocol, read through the eyes of a single molecule: glycine. It never changes shape. What changes is who it meets in the room, and that is enough to change what it does.

Glycine, H₂N–CH₂–COOH
The smallest amino acid, and the only one with no side chain
at all, just a second hydrogen. At body pH it sits as a
zwitterion: +H₃N–CH₂–COO⁻, positive and negative ends
on the same tiny molecule.

The everyday version of this puzzle

salt, water, watermelon

Before glycine, a smaller example, because the underlying idea is the same one people already know from the kitchen.

Salt in water

NaCl → Na⁺ + Cl⁻

The tongue's sodium channels bind Na⁺ directly. Signal in, one flavour out: salty. Nothing about the ion itself is unusual here, it is doing the one job it was invited to do.

Salt in watermelon

NaCl → Na⁺ + Cl⁻ (same ions)

Same dissociation, but now the Na⁺ arrives alongside the fruit's own sugars and a trace of bitterness. Sodium quiets the bitter receptors, and the sweet receptors, no longer competing with that signal, read louder. The ion did not change. The room it landed in did.

Worth being precise about No new compound forms in the watermelon case. This is a receptor-level effect (sodium suppressing bitter signalling, which unmasks sweetness), not a chemical transformation. The analogy is about context changing an outcome, not about salt literally becoming sugar. Held to that standard, it maps onto glycine's story remarkably well.

Meet the molecule at the centre

what L-glycine is already doing, before it meets anything else

Glycine is not a specialist. It is one of the few metabolites the body uses in almost every compartment it has: the gastrointestinal tract, the liver, the nervous system, the skin, and the mitochondria all reach for it. Four of its standing jobs matter for what follows.

structural

Collagen's third residue. Collagen repeats a Gly-X-Y triplet down its whole length. Glycine's side chain is just a hydrogen atom, small enough to sit in the crowded core of the triple helix. Swap in any bulkier amino acid and the helix cannot close. Roughly one residue in three of the body's main structural protein is glycine, by architectural necessity, not coincidence.

mucosal

Bile conjugation. The liver attaches glycine to bile acids before they are released into the gastrointestinal tract, which is what makes bile soluble enough to emulsify dietary fat in the first place.

Cholic acid-CoA + Glycine →(BAAT enzyme)→ Glycocholic acid + CoA-SH
nervous system

Two separate receptor jobs. In the spinal cord and brainstem, glycine opens its own chloride channel and inhibits the neuron, a calming signal. Separately, at the NMDA receptor, glycine has to sit in a co-agonist pocket on the NR1 subunit before that channel will open at all, even with glutamate present. Same molecule, two different seats at two different tables.

detox

The last step of glutathione. The body's main antioxidant is built from three amino acids, and glycine supplies the final one.

L-Glutamate + L-Cysteine →(GCL, ATP)→ γ-Glutamylcysteine
γ-Glutamylcysteine + Glycine →(GS, ATP)→ Glutathione (GSH)

None of these four jobs compete for the same molecule of glycine in a literal sense, the body simply keeps enough in circulation to run all four pools at once. What the protocol below does is decide, phase by phase, which of these four rooms gets extra reinforcement from the supplements standing next to glycine.

The journey through the gastrointestinal tract

what the base layer does, stage by stage, before either phase's add-ons even enter the picture

Digestive enzymes, probiotics, curry leaf, ginger, and Unienzyme are present in both phases and never rotate out. That is because they are not working on one target, they are working on the whole length of the gastrointestinal tract at once, stomach to large intestine, and glycine has a seat at nearly every stage of that route.

Digestive Enzymes Probiotics Curry Leaf Ginger Unienzyme L-Glycine
bile

Glycine makes bile usable. The liver will not release a bile acid into the small intestine until it is conjugated, almost always to glycine or taurine. Ginger adds a mild choleretic push of its own, encouraging the liver to secrete more bile and the gallbladder to contract more readily, so the pool of bile arriving downstream is both larger and, thanks to glycine, properly solubilised.

Cholic acid-CoA + Glycine →(BAAT)→ Glycocholic acid + CoA-SH
Glycocholate: water-soluble enough to emulsify dietary fat into micelles
hydrochloric acid

The proton pump, and where glycine stands guard. Parietal cells in the stomach wall generate acid by pumping H⁺ into the lumen in exchange for K⁺, then letting Cl⁻ follow through a separate channel to complete the hydrochloric acid. That acid is necessary (it activates pepsin and kills ingested microbes) and corrosive at the same time, which is why glycine's collagen and glutathione roles from the section above matter here specifically: they are part of what keeps the mucosal barrier intact against the very acid the stomach is deliberately making.

CO₂ + H₂O →(carbonic anhydrase)→ H₂CO₃ → H⁺ + HCO₃⁻
H⁺ →(H⁺/K⁺-ATPase, the proton pump)→ gastric lumen, exchanged for K⁺
H⁺ + Cl⁻ (via a chloride channel) → HCl (gastric lumen)
enzyme hydrolysis

Where free glycine partly comes from in the first place. Once past the stomach, digestive enzymes take over: Unienzyme's fungal diastase and papain hydrolyse starch to maltose and dietary protein to peptides and free amino acids, right alongside the body's own pancreatic amylase, protease, and lipase. Some of the glycine released from ordinary dietary protein at this step joins the same circulating pool that the supplement is topping up, they are two sources feeding one pool, not two separate stories.

Starch + H₂O →(amylase / diastase)→ Maltose → Glucose
Dietary protein + H₂O →(papain / proteases)→ Peptides + free amino acids (incl. Glycine)
Triglyceride + H₂O →(lipase, needs bile)→ Fatty acids + Glycerol
small intestine villi

Absorption, and one more structural job for glycine. The villi and their microvilli are what turn a simple tube into the body's largest absorptive surface, and glycine crosses into the enterocyte through dedicated amino acid transporters at that brush border. Underneath, the same Gly-X-Y collagen architecture from earlier forms part of the basement membrane the villi are built on, so glycine is doing double duty here: it is one of the things being absorbed, and one of the things the absorbing structure is made of.

Glycine (lumen) →(PAT1 / amino acid transporter, brush border)→ Glycine (enterocyte) → portal blood
large intestine, short-chain fatty acids

The probiotic layer's payoff further down. Whatever fibre from curry leaf and the diet escapes digestion in the small intestine reaches the large intestine, where the probiotic bacteria ferment it anaerobically into short-chain fatty acids. Butyrate, the main one, is the preferred fuel for the colon's own lining cells and is linked to a calmer, better-sealed colonic barrier, a large-intestine parallel to the small-intestine mucosal support glycine and zinc carnosine provide further up.

Resistant starch / fibre →(anaerobic fermentation, probiotic bacteria)→ Acetate + Propionate + Butyrate + CO₂ + H₂
Butyrate → primary fuel for colonocyte ATP production
Read of the base layer None of these five stages depend on which phase is running. Bile conjugation, acid buffering, hydrolysis, absorption, and fermentation are the constant infrastructure of the gastrointestinal tract, and glycine shows up in three of the five (bile, the mucosal barrier against acid, and the villi's structure) before either phase's specialised add-ons are even considered. The phases decide which of glycine's roles gets extra reinforcement. This section is why glycine was already indispensable before that decision was made.

The twenty days, in two phases

same base layer throughout, one set of add-ons rotates

Every set below sits on the same digestive foundation: digestive enzymes, probiotics, curry leaf, ginger, and Unienzyme, taken across the day's meals. That base layer does not change and is not itself the story here, it is the constant room that everything else operates inside. The chips marked in dark are the ones doing the interesting ionic work.

Phase one

Settle and calm

Days 1 to 10 · eight pills across three sittings

Digestive Enzymes Probiotics L-Glycine Curry Leaf Ginger Unienzyme L-Theanine ALCAR + Magnesium L-Threonate + NAC
detox

NAC feeds the glutathione line directly. NAC is deacetylated in the body to cysteine, the rate-limiting raw material in the equation above. Add NAC, and glycine's detox pathway stops being limited by cysteine supply. This is the mucosal antioxidant layer for a gastrointestinal lining that is actively being asked to heal.

nervous system

L-theanine and glycine point the same direction. L-theanine resembles glutamate closely enough to nudge GABA signalling upward and raise the brain's calmer alpha-wave activity. Glycine, at the same time, is opening its own inhibitory chloride channel in the spinal cord and brainstem. Two different receptors, one shared outcome: a nervous system that is easier to quiet.

mitochondrial

ALCAR pays the energy bill. Acetyl-L-carnitine ferries fatty acids across the mitochondrial membrane for beta-oxidation, and gastrointestinal epithelial cells, which turn over every few days, are among the most energy-hungry tissue in the body. It can also donate its acetyl group toward acetylcholine synthesis in nerve tissue.

Acetyl-CoA (from ALCAR) + Choline →(ChAT)→ Acetylcholine + CoA-SH
nervous system

Magnesium sits inside the very channel glycine helps gate. At rest, an Mg²⁺ ion physically blocks the NMDA channel pore. Magnesium L-threonate is the one magnesium salt shown to meaningfully raise brain magnesium levels, so this add-on is reinforcing the same receptor complex glycine already has a foothold in, from a different pocket on it.

Read of the phase Everything added in the first ten days points at calming the nervous system and re-supplying the antioxidant pathway. The gastrointestinal tract is the stated target, but the two add-ons chosen (NAC, Mg L-threonate) do their most direct work in glutathione chemistry and NMDA receptor tone, both of which glycine was already sitting inside.

Phase two

Rebuild the surface

Days 11 to 20 · seven pills across three sittings

Digestive Enzymes Probiotics L-Glycine Curry Leaf Ginger Unienzyme ALCAR L-Theanine + Vitamin B12 + NAC + Zinc Carnosine
mucosal

Zinc carnosine works on the tissue directly, not through glycine. Zinc and the dipeptide carnosine form a stable chelate that adheres to the gastric lining, stabilises the epithelial membrane, and scavenges free radicals right at the site of irritation. It is a topical-style repair agent layered on top of glycine's own collagen-building role, the two are reinforcing the same wall from different angles.

Zn²⁺ + L-Carnosine (β-alanyl-L-histidine) → Zn-carnosine chelate
structural

Glycine's collagen job is now the one being leaned on. With L-theanine gone, the calming emphasis of phase one steps back, and glycine's structural role (the Gly-X-Y triplet from earlier) becomes the more relevant of its four jobs as the tissue moves from being calmed to being rebuilt.

nervous system

B12 hands the methylation cycle its methyl group. Methylcobalamin is the cofactor methionine synthase needs to pull this reaction off. The methionine produced feeds one-carbon metabolism broadly, including the maintenance of the enteric nervous system, the gastrointestinal tract's own semi-independent nerve network that governs motility.

Homocysteine + 5-methyl-THF →(methionine synthase, B12)→ Methionine + THF
detox

NAC carries over unchanged. The glutathione line opened in phase one is not a ten-day job, so its raw material supply continues.

Read of the phase The center of gravity shifts from calming and antioxidant defence toward direct structural repair and methylation support. Zinc carnosine and glycine's collagen role now do adjacent work on the same wall, and B12 extends that repair signal into the nerves that line the gastrointestinal tract, which is a different room from where phase one's effort was spent.

Why the gastrointestinal protocol shows up elsewhere

This is the part that is easy to misread as the supplement "spreading" into unrelated systems. It is closer to the opposite: the gastrointestinal tract was never glycine's only room, it was simply the room this protocol chose to knock on. Skin, sleep architecture, and general antioxidant capacity all sit downstream of the same four pathways above, so reinforcing glycine's supply and its supporting cofactors for gastrointestinal repair tends to nudge those other rooms as a side effect of using the same molecule, not because a second mechanism was introduced.

That is the practical reason a molecule like this is worth building a protocol around in the first place: it is not that it does one thing well, it is that it does four things adequately, all the time, and a well-chosen set of neighbours decides which of the four gets amplified this week.

The one idea worth keeping

Glycine did not change shape once across either phase of this protocol. What changed, each time, was who was standing next to it.

Salt

Same Na⁺ and Cl⁻ ions. Water reads them as salty, fruit reads them as a sweetness amplifier.

Phase one

NAC and magnesium L-threonate pull glycine's detox and NMDA-adjacent roles forward. The nervous system settles.

Phase two

Zinc carnosine and B12 pull glycine's structural and methylation roles forward. The tissue rebuilds.
